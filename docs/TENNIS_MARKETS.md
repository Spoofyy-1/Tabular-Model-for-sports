# Tennis market timing and historical quote audit

Research date: September 25, 2026. Research and proposal only: no tennis quote files were downloaded or processed locally, no trading occurred, and no tennis execution backtest has been established. Any collection below must run on a genuine GitHub-hosted runner. Azure training remains a separate, explicitly authorized experiment.

Point sequences can support a model of the probability of winning the next point, game, set or match conditional on the score and server. They cannot establish profitability from entering after a serve unless we know when the serve or point became observable and which executable prices remained available after the resulting decision and exchange delay. Scheduled match time plus point number is insufficient.

## Delays and pauses that affect simulated entries

Polymarket's current developer documentation says configured sports markets use a market-specific delay window around live conditions. A marketable order cannot be cancelled while pending; checks run again before matching and can reject it. The documentation does not establish one universal historical tennis delay. Record the effective configuration; an unknown delay is not zero. [Official order lifecycle](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/order-lifecycle).

An April 2026 help page describes three-second sports marketable-order delays, with a one-second experiment in NBA/MLB, and says outstanding orders are cleared at game start while warning that schedule changes can affect clearing. Treat this as dated product guidance, not proof that every tennis order had that delay or that a particular book was cleared. [Official limit-order guidance](https://help.polymarket.com/en/articles/13364444-limit-orders).

Kalshi distinguishes an active market from an inactive/paused one. Reopening an exchange-deactivated market cancels resting orders. Scheduled close/expiry fields can differ from event time, and some lifecycle transitions occur without a WebSocket activation event. Capture status changes and close-time revisions alongside books. [Market lifecycle](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/market_lifecycle). Exchange-wide maintenance pauses also affect order acceptance; cancellation availability differs between a trading pause and an exchange pause. [Maintenance and pauses](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/maintenance_and_pauses). No reviewed primary source establishes that all Kalshi or Polymarket tennis books suspend after every serve or point.

Kalshi's historical candlestick API offers minute-scale aggregation, including one-minute bars. A bar cannot reveal the quote available immediately after a specific serve, queue priority, or depth through the delay window. [Historical candlesticks](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/historical/get-historical-market-candlesticks).

Polymarket's market WebSocket includes timestamped book, change, trade and lifecycle events. Its sports stream includes score/status fields but warns that information can lag or omit events; the documented general fields do not establish complete tennis serve-by-serve wall clocks. It is useful as a diagnostic source, not an assumed authoritative point clock. [Official real-time data documentation](https://docs.polymarket.com/market-data/realtime-data).

## Bounded cloud collection candidate

The strongest quote lead is [PMXT's Polymarket v2 archive](https://archive.pmxt.dev/Polymarket/v2). Its [schema documentation](https://archive.pmxt.dev/docs/v2-data-overview) identifies public hourly Parquet files from April 13, 2026, with source and collector-receipt UTC timestamps, condition IDs, decimal-string outcome token IDs, snapshots, deltas and trades. Snapshot bid/ask arrays contain price and size; most rows are deltas, not complete books. A documented example is `https://r2v2.pmxt.dev/polymarket_orderbook_2026-04-17T12.parquet`. The documented typical size is 100–400 MB, although current index entries can exceed that range. Preserve timestamp precision and original event type; compute snapshot best prices from actual arrays. This schema provides a plausible route to historical depth, not a verified tennis dataset.

The archive is distributed under CC BY 4.0 with attribution to PMXT. The [PendulumFlow mirror documentation](https://archive.pendulumflow.com/formats/v2) retains that license and describes coverage gaps and schema issues. Its measured incomplete coverage is a reason to audit each requested asset/hour rather than rely on a claim of universal coverage. PMXT's separately hosted API terms are not the archive's license. Public outputs should include the license, attribution, original archive URL, SHA-256 and transformation description. Keep full venue rule text out of public exports; retain rule URLs and hashes unless redistribution rights are established separately.

## What the point feed must supply

For an entry after a completed point, require stable match/player IDs, tournament, round, surface, server, point ordinal, score before/after, point winner, source event timestamp, source publication timestamp when available, collector receipt timestamp, sequence/revision identifier and explicit timezone. An entry after the first serve instead requires a distinct first-serve/fault event and its timing; the final point winner alone cannot reconstruct that decision moment.

Join by unique players plus tournament, round and date, preserving source IDs. Report unresolved identity conflicts. Do not estimate point timestamps by evenly spreading points over match duration or fitting point order to price moves. Such alignment would introduce invented observations and can select the future quote that makes a strategy appear successful.

A later execution experiment must make information available no earlier than its receipt time, then account for model computation, network delay and the applicable exchange hold. Value a buy against available asks and depth at the resulting matching time, with the effective fee schedule, market state and stale-data rules. Last trade, midpoint and pre-point quotes are not assumed fill prices. A maker-fill model additionally needs queue evidence; touching a limit is insufficient. Synthetic latency scenarios should be labelled assumptions, not measured execution. Delayed score feeds and broadcast clocks need their own lag audit.

Until these conditions are met, the useful deliverable is a probability model with chronological, match-grouped evaluation. Estimate clutch performance conditional on score, server, surface, opponent and prior ability; shrink player effects with few critical points. Being down early is already reflected in the match state and usually in prices, so raw comeback frequency is not itself a pricing edge. Fit player histories only from earlier matches, keep every point of a match in the same chronological partition, and treat future point/match outcomes only as labels. Evaluate calibration, log loss and Brier score first. Profitability remains a separate empirical question.
